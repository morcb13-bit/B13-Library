How it works

Every number is kept as a fraction, integer ÷ integer. 0.1 is 1/10 and 0.2 is 1/5. Their sum is 3/10, so the answer is exactly 0.3. (Many calculators and programming languages that use floating point give 0.30000000000000004.)

The only operations inside are adding and comparing. Subtraction flips the sign and adds. Multiplication adds a number to itself to build a doubling ladder and adds up the rungs it needs. Division subtracts rungs of the divisor's ladder from the top while they fit. Fractions are reduced with the Euclidean algorithm. The counter shows the additions needed to reach the fraction; converting it to decimals or balanced digits is not counted. Decimals are truncated toward zero, negative numbers included.

The balanced panel writes the same answer in balanced ternary (−1, 0, +1) and balanced base 13 (−6 … +6). Answers with decimals get a radix point there too, down to the same precision as the decimal digits you chose.

Numbers whose decimals never end, such as √2, π, EXP, LN and SIN, are computed with 12 extra digits as scaled integers, cut off, and tagged approximate. If any approximation enters a formula, the answer is approximate. Answers that are fractions are tagged exact; repeating decimals show the repeating part in parentheses.
